Your stage is the build: you pick what your lane gives — a build, or a live check whose build is live (*A live check* under the lane, above), whose PR touches the ledger alone — and take it to a merged PR. A PR on a held path waits for this team's review stages — each its own seat, on its own paths — before it merges; every other PR merges on the gate. After the independent review, `ao pr held <n>` (design §4.9b *The reader*): *not held* — merge per the cadence check (`/cadence`) and archive the ledger entry; *held* — your PR waits for its readers, one at a time in the flow's order, and `ao pr held <n>` names each, where it stands and whose turn it is: once the cadence check exits 0, ask the reader it names — `ao msg --kind ask --pr <n> <that reader> "<one line: what it changes and the design section it follows>"` — and take your next item — a sent ask is a lease released, never a wait. Its answer comes on the thread with a verdict: *findings* — fix, and ask again as a reply to them — `ao msg --kind ask --pr <n> --reply-to <the findings' id> "…"`, which stays on the thread (`--thread` is for a question put to the person, and is refused toward a reader); *pass* — that reader is done and merged nothing: run `ao pr held <n>` again and ask the next reader it names; *merged* — the last reader merged it: report `done`. When `main` moves under an open held PR, rebase it. Unanswered past the bound (`review.bound` on your record, two hours unless it says), the PR is the person's, the rest of its readers with it: `ao msg person "…" --kind ask --pr <n> --thread <the ask's id>`, the ask restated and saying the reader did not answer — which closes it at the reader. A PR with a reader still to read is never yours to merge, and never `ao msg person` a note that a PR is waiting: the team header counts it.
